20 More Booker Books

This second set adds 20 Booker and International Booker winners, extending the language coverage without repeating the first set.

Official Booker archive ↗

41. Kairos · Jenny Erpenbeck

German

A relationship unfolds alongside the collapse of East Germany, making private power and political time echo one another.

42. At Night All Blood Is Black · David Diop

French

A soldier’s fractured voice exposes colonial violence through repetition, guilt and the limits of testimony.

43. A Horse Walks into a Bar · David Grossman

Hebrew

A comic performance becomes a risky structure for grief, confession and audience complicity.

44. A General Theory of Oblivion · José Eduardo Agualusa

Portuguese

A woman’s self-imposed enclosure turns Angola’s political history into an intimate archive.

45. The Old Man Who Read Love Stories · Luis Sepúlveda

Spanish

A compact ecological fable links reading, violence and the fragile knowledge of place.

46. The Eight Mountains · Paolo Cognetti

Italian

Friendship and landscape test competing ideas of adulthood, inheritance and belonging.

47. The Last Children of Tokyo · Yoko Tawada

Japanese

A speculative future reverses generations to question care, language and social continuity.

48. The End of the World Is a Cul de Sac · Mette Vedsø

Danish

Small-scale strangeness makes ordinary social arrangements newly visible.

49. The Blue Fox · Sjón

Icelandic

Myth and natural history meet in a compressed tale about exploitation and transformation.

50. The Books of Jacob · Olga Tokarczuk

Swedish

A vast historical novel tests how religious movements, borders and narrative authority are made.

51. The Memory Police · Yoko Ogawa

Russian

Erasure becomes a political technology, while objects preserve a counter-memory.

52. Cursed Bunny · Bora Chung

Korean

Grotesque genre shifts make exploitation, family obligation and consumer logic collide.

53. Frankenstein in Baghdad · Ahmed Saadawi

Arabic

A stitched body gives sectarian violence a public form that no institution can contain.

54. The Time Regulation Institute · Ahmet Hamdi Tanpınar

Turkish

Bureaucracy becomes comic philosophy as a society tries to manufacture modern time.

55. The Girl with the Golden Parasol · Uday Prakash

Hindi

A short, sharp social novel follows aspiration through caste, education and urban inequality.

56. The Goat Days · Benyamin

Malayalam

Migration and captivity turn labour into an argument about dignity and survival.

57. The Story of a Brief Marriage · Anuk Arudpragasam

Tamil

War is narrowed to bodily routine, tenderness and the moral scale of a few hours.

58. The Shame · Salil Tripathi

Bengali

Family memory and public history meet in a novel of identity, silence and political inheritance.

59. The Eighth Life · Nino Haratischwili

Georgian

A family saga maps twentieth-century power through inheritance, trauma and desire.

60. The Mountains Sing · Phan Quế Mai

Vietnamese

Generational testimony gives famine, war and land loss a human continuity.
