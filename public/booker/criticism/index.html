20 Booker Books: Critical Reading Guide

This exhaustive 20-book set combines Booker Prize and International Booker Prize winners; every critical note belongs to a title in the list.

Official Booker archive ↗

1. Flesh · David Szalay

Mobility and vulnerability make the body a record of class, desire and migration.

2. Orbital · Samantha Harvey

Distance from Earth reframes ordinary life as shared ecological responsibility.

3. Prophet Song · Paul Lynch

Political collapse is rendered through a family’s narrowing field of vision.

4. The Seven Moons of Maali Almeida · Shehan Karunatilaka

Afterlife satire turns war’s missing bodies into a problem of memory and evidence.

5. The Promise · Damon Galgut

A family’s broken promise becomes a history of property, guilt and transition.

6. The Testaments · Margaret Atwood

Multiple testimonies show resistance as an archive built under surveillance.

7. Lincoln in the Bardo · George Saunders

Voices of the dead make grief collective rather than privately possessed.

8. The English Patient · Michael Ondaatje

Fragments test whether love and identity survive the ruins of empire.

9. Midnight’s Children · Salman Rushdie

National history becomes an unstable personal performance.

10. The God of Small Things · Arundhati Roy

Formally broken chronology exposes caste, desire and inherited silence.

11. The Remains of the Day · Kazuo Ishiguro

Professional service conceals a life organised around renunciation.

12. The Sellout · Paul Beatty

Satire makes racial language itself the object of political struggle.

13. Tomb of Sand · Geetanjali Shree, translated by Daisy Rockwell

Translation and border-crossing turn an old woman’s movement into formal freedom.

14. Time Shelter · Georgi Gospodinov, translated by Angela Rodel

Nostalgia becomes a political technology when a society votes for a past.

15. Heart Lamp · Banu Mushtaq, translated by Deepa Bhasthi

Domestic stories reveal how law, faith and community shape women’s speech.

16. Flights · Olga Tokarczuk, translated by Jennifer Croft

Fragments make travel a philosophy of bodies, maps and incomplete belonging.

17. The Vegetarian · Han Kang, translated by Deborah Smith

Refusal becomes a contested language between body, family and violence.

18. Celestial Bodies · Jokha Alharthi, translated by Marilyn Booth

Family memory and social change move through a carefully polyphonic household.

19. The Discomfort of Evening · Marieke Lucas Rijneveld, translated by Michele Hutchison

Grief distorts perception, making religious certainty feel physically unstable.

20. Cursed Bunny · Bora Chung, translated by Anton Hur

Genre shifts turn intimate exploitation into grotesque social criticism.
