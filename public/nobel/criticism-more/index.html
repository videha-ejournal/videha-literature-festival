20 More Nobel Books

This is the second exhaustive set: 20 additional books by Nobel Literature laureates, selected to extend language coverage without repeating the first set.

Official Nobel laureate archive ↗

21. The Lover · Marguerite Duras

French

A compressed memory novel where desire, class and colonial history shape one another.

22. Death in Venice · Thomas Mann

German

Artistic idealisation turns beauty into a study of fear, discipline and collapse.

23. The Feast of the Goat · Mario Vargas Llosa

Spanish

A political novel showing dictatorship through memory, witness and fractured time.

24. Six Characters in Search of an Author · Luigi Pirandello

Italian

The unfinished characters make theatrical form itself the subject of the drama.

25. One Day in the Life of Ivan Denisovich · Aleksandr Solzhenitsyn

Russian

Routine and measured attention become forms of survival inside the camp system.

26. Drive Your Plow Over the Bones of the Dead · Olga Tokarczuk

Polish

Crime, ecology and unreliable perspective unsettle the boundary between justice and obsession.

27. Red Sorghum · Mo Yan

Chinese

Family saga and sensory violence turn rural history into a contested national memory.

28. Snow Country · Yasunari Kawabata

Japanese

Sparse beauty exposes distance, performance and the loneliness of desire.

29. Snow · Orhan Pamuk

Turkish

A provincial city becomes a stage for secularism, faith, politics and representation.

30. Midaq Alley · Naguib Mahfouz

Arabic

One street gathers the pressure of modernisation, aspiration and unequal power.

31. Only Yesterday · S. Y. Agnon

Hebrew

A migrant’s story makes language, settlement and historical memory inseparable.

32. Gösta Berling’s Saga · Selma Lagerlöf

Swedish

Legendary storytelling turns moral failure and community into a generous epic.

33. The Year of the Death of Ricardo Reis · José Saramago

Portuguese

A dead poet’s return lets history, dictatorship and literary identity speak together.

34. Fatelessness · Imre Kertész

Hungarian

Plain narration refuses to make catastrophe morally comfortable or narratively familiar.

35. Axion Esti · Odysseas Elytis

Greek

Poetry joins personal memory, landscape and national history in a liturgical structure.

36. Hunger · Knut Hamsun

Norwegian

Interior monologue turns poverty into a volatile experiment in consciousness.

37. Human Acts · Han Kang

Korean

Linked voices make state violence a problem of bodies, testimony and mourning.

38. The Hunger Angel · Herta Müller

Romanian

Deportation is rendered through objects, hunger and the grammar of survival.

39. The Plague Column · Jaroslav Seifert

Czech

Lyric memory makes public history intimate without reducing it to nostalgia.

40. The Bridge on the Drina · Ivo Andrić

Serbian

A bridge becomes the durable witness through which empire, violence and coexistence are narrated.
